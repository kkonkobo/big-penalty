# Open-source job agents leave France and the US open

The ai-job-search repo is not an application but a bundle of Claude Code slash commands, skills, subagent prompts, small Python helpers and LaTeX templates. It has **72,195 stars and 23,216 forks** and stops deliberately before submitting anything ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/MadsLorentzen/ai-job-search)). Its closest peer is career-ops (**83,211 stars**), which uses the same "human presses Submit" stance ([career-ops](https://github.com/career-ops-hq/career-ops)). Around these two sit autonomous auto-apply bots (AIHawk, ApplyPilot, GodsScion), scrapers (JobSpy), resume tools (Resume-Matcher, Reactive Resume, RenderCV) and trackers (JobSync). The forks are mostly localizations: China, Brazil, Spain, Canada, the UK and others. **No French fork and no real US fork turned up**, and no maintained French portal connector exists. For France, the main constraints are that the France Travail API is closed to new users (as of 19 Oct 2026), that Welcome to the Jungle's terms prohibit scraping, and that French CV, letter and contract conventions are barely modelled. For the US, the open space is a maintained ATS-connector layer (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday) with pay-range parsing, sponsorship filtering and truthful self-ID handling. The legal risk sits in contract and account bans, not in the criminal law on computer access. Star counts are popularity signals, not quality signals; I did not audit their growth. Coverage of the long tail is incomplete because the GitHub API was rate-limited partway through the research, and about 16,000 forks were not scanned.

## The reference repo is a prompt framework, and its forks are mostly localizations

ai-job-search runs the pipeline setup, scrape, rank, apply, interview and outcome tracking. There are 14 commands in total, six of them core. The profile lives in `CLAUDE.md` with placeholders that `/setup` fills in. Subagents handle posting fetching, scoring and review, and nine methodology files cover profile, writing style, evaluation, CV and cover-letter templates, interview prep and forms ([README](https://github.com/ai-job-search-dev/ai-job-search#readme)). The `/apply` step is a drafter-reviewer loop. A deterministic gate first checks postings for hidden text and injected instructions. Fit is then scored across five dimensions, including a language gate. A fresh-context reviewer agent researches the company and critiques the CV and letter drafts, which are LaTeX documents compiled to PDF. An ATS text-layer check follows, and claims are verified against the profile, with no fabrication. Storage is flat files (markdown, JSON, CSV), the UI is the CLI, and the tool needs a Claude subscription or API credits. Codex and Gemini CLI are supported through a pointer file. The cost model is therefore the user's own tokens, and the "database" is a folder.

Its market origin is Denmark. Until PR #829 on 2026-11-16 (v2.0.0, a breaking change), Danish boards such as Jobindex and Jobnet shipped in the core. Portals now live in a separate community-portals repo (8 stars), which has only `dk` and `global` folders, the latter with LinkedIn and freehire search. There is no France folder ([community-portals](https://github.com/ai-job-search-dev/community-portals)). Scraping falls back to LLM web search unless a portal skill is installed. Portal skills are Bun/TypeScript CLIs with a `search`/`detail` contract, so a new French or US connector is a bounded task and not a fork of the core. The maintainer's curated fork index (pinned Discussion #78) states that market-specific portals belong in forks, and that any fork that fills or submits applications must have an unconditional human confirmation and no captcha bypass ([Discussion #78](https://github.com/ai-job-search-dev/ai-job-search/discussions/78)).

Of roughly 23,000 forks, most are untouched one-click copies. The notable ones are real localizations, summarized below (commit counts from the GitHub compare API).

## The wider field splits into copilots, auto-appliers, scrapers, resume tools and trackers

Auto-apply bots came in three generations. First came Selenium LinkedIn Easy Apply bots: GodsScion (3,045 stars, still active), wodsuz (839, now the free tier of a commercial product) and several stale repos. Second came LLM-driven browser agents that work on any ATS form: ApplyPilot (1,836 stars, AGPL-3.0, no pushes since March 2026) and the rewritten AIHawk (32,574 stars). Third came human-in-the-loop career agents shipped as agent skills: career-ops, OfferOS and JobAppAgent ([ApplyPilot](https://github.com/Pickle-Pixel/ApplyPilot), [AIHawk](https://github.com/feder-cr/Jobs_Applier_AI_Agent), [GodsScion](https://github.com/GodsScion/Auto_job_applier_linkedIn)). The current AIHawk searches 37 public sources, scores postings 1 to 5, then drives a real Firefox to fill and submit the company's own form. Its own status table shows what works. Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever, JazzHR and BambooHR are "sent and confirmed on a real form". JazzHR and BambooHR are blocked by reCAPTCHA image puzzles on real postings, and Personio stops at a captcha about half the time ([docs/sites.md](https://github.com/feder-cr/Jobs_Applier_AI_Agent/blob/main/docs/sites.md)). The newer copilots draw the opposite line. career-ops says its script "never POSTs", fills forms and "stops before Submit", and calls itself "a filter, not a spray-and-pray auto-applier". OfferOS enforces the submit gate in tool code instead of in prompts ([OfferOS](https://github.com/averatec0773/offeros)). My reading is that this shift responds to ToS, ban risk and spam backlash (The Verge, Semafor and 404 Media are cited in AIHawk's README), though only career-ops states the reasoning. I found no evidence of a legal takedown of any of these repos, and the usual fates are commercial pivot, rewrite or abandonment.

The building blocks are well differentiated. JobSpy (4,751 stars, MIT, active) is the dominant scraper library, with outputs as a pandas DataFrame, a CLI and an MCP server. It covers LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, ZipRecruiter, Bayt, Naukri, BDJobs and HelloWork. Its README says Google Jobs is currently unavailable ([JobSpy](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy)). Most boards cap results at about 1,000 per search, and LinkedIn returns 429 on bursts. JobFunnel is archived. ATS aggregation is handled inside other projects: JobSync discovers jobs from Greenhouse (2,400+ companies), Lever (1,160+) and Ashby (1,860+) without API keys, while agentic-career-search claims 119 adapters (self-reported, 102 stars) ([JobSync](https://github.com/Gsync/jobsync), [agentic-career-search](https://github.com/Francis1998/agentic-career-search)). For resumes, Resume-Matcher (29,320 stars) offers FastAPI, Next.js and SQLite with many LLM providers plus Ollama, no-LLM ATS scoring and a French UI locale ([Resume-Matcher](https://github.com/srbhr/Resume-Matcher)). Reactive Resume (45,851 stars) is a Postgres-backed builder with an AI assistant ([Reactive Resume](https://github.com/reactive-resume/reactive-resume)). RenderCV (17,965 stars) turns YAML into PDFs through Typst and has a French locale file ([RenderCV](https://github.com/rendercv/rendercv)). OpenResume is U.S.-centric and stale, with its last commit in October 2024 ([OpenResume](https://github.com/xitanggg/open-resume)). JobSync (1,532 stars) is the only widely used self-hosted tracker with AI matching, on Next.js and Postgres with pgvector, supporting Ollama and cloud models ([JobSync](https://github.com/Gsync/jobsync)).

One architectural finding matters for design. Real standalone embedding-based matching repos with adoption did not appear. Scoring is either an LLM rubric (career-ops, ai-job-search), keyword and deterministic scoring (Resume-Matcher's ATS score, agentic-career-search), or a hybrid in which a cheap local relevance score filters and an LLM scores only the top candidates (JobSync). The common stack is therefore a job feed (JobSpy or ATS JSON), an LLM scorer, a tailoring step, an optional form-filler and a tracker. The weak spots in the field are scheduling, notifications and non-English conventions. ai-job-search has no scheduler and no push alerts in core. No standalone French daily digest with LLM scoring was found. The two giants are also prompt frameworks, so their French or US behavior is whatever the prompts and portals say, and not enforced code.

## France: no connector, a closed API and a thin localization layer

The only official, free French job API is France Travail's Offres d'emploi v2. It limits each query to 1,150 results and allows 10 calls per second ([data.gouv.fr](https://www.data.gouv.fr/dataservices/api-offres-demploi)). A third-party write-up says the OAuth scope is `api_offresdemploiv2` and that queries must be sliced by date, department or ROME code to get around the cap ([jobspipe.dev](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/france-travail-api)). The decisive finding is recent. On 19 Oct 2026, in the data.gouv.fr discussion thread, France Travail wrote that access is "actuellement suspendu pour les nouveaux requérants". It said existing grants are not called into question "at this stage", and that reuse of API data in AI services is among the things being examined. The API page itself cites "usages automatisés massifs par certaines solutions d'intelligence artificielle" ([discussions](https://www.data.gouv.fr/dataservices/api-offres-demploi/discussions)). I found no later update. A new open-source tool therefore cannot assume self-service keys. It should ship the connector as bring-your-own-credentials and off by default, poll politely, and honor the licence as described by third parties: display full offer content and logo, with attribution (not verified against the official terms). That condition may clash with an AI tool that summarizes or reformats offers. Existing OSS wrappers are tiny: cmnemoi/france-travail-api (0 stars) and a France Travail MCP server (2 stars) ([GitHub search](https://github.com/search?q=france+travail+api&type=repositories)).

The remaining French sources are better ordered by legal safety than by audience. La Bonne Alternance's API is free with an account, supports searching apprenticeship offers by ROME code, RNCP and radius, and includes an endpoint to send a candidate application. That endpoint should stay user-confirmed ([data.gouv.fr](https://www.data.gouv.fr/dataservices/672cf64ff8b5d52b76263394)). Choisir le service public publishes an open CSV under Licence Ouverte 2.0, updated weekly, though a user comment says it holds very little information per offer (secondhand, unread). The DGAFP reported 347,200 public-sector offers published in 2025 ([Acteurs publics](https://acteurspublics.fr/articles/347-000-offres-demploi-publiees-sur-choisir-le-service-public-en-2025/)). Welcome to the Jungle exposes a public Algolia search endpoint according to third-party write-ups, but its terms (Partie A, art. 10) prohibit scraping, automated extraction and copying, and art. 19.2 limits use to personal purposes ([WTTJ terms](https://www.welcometothejungle.com/fr/pages/terms)). The only WTTJ scrapers on GitHub are repos with 0 to 2 stars. APEC has no official API, and Apify actors say its internal search endpoint is reachable while detail pages sit behind DataDome ([Apify](https://apify.com/unfenced-group/apec-fr-scraper)). Indeed.fr, Glassdoor FR and HelloWork are reachable through JobSpy, which defaults `country_indeed` to the USA. HelloWork searches France only and works best with French terms ([JobSpy](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy)). LinkedIn's User Agreement section 8.2 bars scrapers, bots and browser plugins ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)). I found no access path for Cadremploi, Jobteaser, Malt or Free-Work. My recommended default is a paste-URL or user-browser flow, as career-ops uses, plus La Bonne Alternance, CSP and per-company ATS boards. Whether French employers publish on Greenhouse, Lever or Workable boards was not verified for France specifically. Hiring conditions raise the stakes of good filtering: Indeed Hiring Lab reports French posting volume 5.8% below February 2020 at end of May 2026 ([Hiring Lab](https://www.hiringlab.org/fr/blog/2026/06/24/panorama-du-marche-du-travail-europeen-juin-2026/)). I found no source ranking boards by cadre versus non-cadre use, so which boards to prioritize for which profile is my inference.

Localization is where career-ops leads, but only partly. Its `modes/fr/` folder has five files (README, shared rules, offre, pipeline, postuler), and it covers CDI and CDD, période d'essai, statut cadre, Syntec, RTT, 13th month, intéressement, titres-restaurant, mutuelle and portage salarial, plus brut annuel salary negotiation ([modes/fr/_shared.md](https://github.com/career-ops-hq/career-ops/blob/main/modes/fr/_shared.md)). My own grep of those files found **no mention of photo, état civil, vous-moi-nous letters, gratification, alternance, intérim, anti-discrimination law, La Bonne Alternance or the France Travail API**. Scan, PDF, tracker and batch modes remain English or Spanish, and the French text is guidance for the model, not enforced code ([modes/fr/README.md](https://github.com/career-ops-hq/career-ops/blob/main/modes/fr/README.md)). The conventions the tool needs are documented elsewhere. A photo is optional and more common in sales, hospitality and large traditional firms than in tech ([Expatica](https://expatica.com/fr/working/finding-a-job/french-cv-interview-tips-101770)). One page suffices for most profiles ([Studi](https://www.studi.com/fr/alternance/4-conseils-pour-rediger-un-cv-qui-retient-lattention-des-recruteurs)). The lettre de motivation follows a "vous / moi / nous" structure, about one page and three to four paragraphs ([JobSeeker](https://www.jobseeker.com/fr/lettre-de-motivation/conseils/lettre-de-motivation-je-vous-nous)). Période d'essai caps for a CDI are 2, 3 and 4 months by category, with one renewal where allowed ([France Travail](https://www.francetravail.fr/employeur/lessentiel-pour-embaucher/les-contrats-de-travail/la-periode-dessai.html), seen only as a search summary). The 2026 stage gratification minimum is 4.50 EUR per hour ([Legisocial](https://www.legisocial.fr/reperes-sociaux/gratification-stagiaire-2026.html)), and sources disagree on whether the two-month threshold must be consecutive. Syntec minimums differ by site and should be checked on code.travail.gouv.fr ([Code du travail numérique](https://code.travail.gouv.fr/contribution/1486-quel-est-le-salaire-minimum)). Article L1132-1 of the Code du travail bars discrimination on listed criteria from hiring onward, and the anonymous CV was never made mandatory ([swim.legal](https://www.swim.legal/blog/motifs-de-discrimination-au-travail-criteres-prohibes-code-travail)). My design inference is that a French default CV should omit age, marital status and nationality, keep the photo optional, and treat contract type as a first-class field (CDI, CDD, intérim, alternance, stage, freelance, portage). On the CV side, RenderCV's French locale and Reactive Resume's multi-language support are the most plausible renderers, but whether either ships a French-style template was not verified. Preferences for French-language output on French postings follow from HelloWork's own guidance and the ai-job-search "language gate" idea.

Legal exposure in France and the EU is mostly GDPR and contract. CNIL's June 2025 practice sheets say scraping is not prohibited per se, requires case-by-case analysis and data minimization (GDPR Art. 5.1.c), and is limited by site terms ([Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/fr/publications/development-of-an-ai-system-cnil-issues-guidelines-regarding-collection-of-data-via-web-scraping)). A candidate-side tool handles the user's own CV and history, so local-first storage and explicit consent before sending data to an LLM provider are the safest design. Employment AI is high-risk under the EU AI Act, and several firms report the Digital Omnibus pushed the Annex III obligations to 2 Dec 2027 ([Cooley](https://cdp.cooley.com/digital-ai-omnibus-delays-key-deadlines-introduces-new-rules/)). A DLA Piper update still advises planning against 2 Aug 2026, so sources conflict ([DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act)). That category targets employer-side screening; whether a candidate-side tool falls within it is my inference (probably not, unless it ranks other people), and I did not read the Act's text. Counsel should confirm.

## US: plenty of access, but the connector layer and candidate logic are unbuilt

The US has the cleanest technical surface. Greenhouse board GETs need no authentication, and `pay_transparency=true` returns structured pay ranges ([Greenhouse](https://docs.greenhouse.io/job-board.html)). Lever's postings API is keyless to read and includes `workplaceType` and an optional `salaryRange`, with an EU endpoint at `api.eu.lever.co` ([Lever](https://github.com/lever/postings-api)). Ashby returns compensation with `includeCompensation=true` ([Ashby](https://developers.ashbyhq.com/docs/public-job-posting-api)). Workable and Recruitee have public endpoints with conflicting claims about whether keys are required. Workday has no official public API. Its undocumented CXS endpoint takes POST requests with a cap of 20 results per page according to third-party posts, so it should be treated as fragile ([DEV](https://dev.to/udaninn/workday-job-boards-have-a-json-api-too-its-just-better-hidden-23fl)). iCIMS has no public feed. USAJOBS requires a free key and supports filters for remote work, security clearance and hiring path ([USAJOBS](https://developer.usajobs.gov/api-reference/get-api-search)). Indeed's publisher API is retired and Glassdoor's partner API is effectively closed, per vendor sources whose closure dates disagree ([100hires](https://100hires.com/indeed-api.html)). Adzuna (which lists `us` and `fr` country codes per an unofficial MCP server), Remotive and the HN API are free fallbacks ([Remotive](https://github.com/remotive-io/remote-jobs-api)). Remotive's terms require attribution and forbid resubmitting jobs to Google Jobs or LinkedIn. The open-source side is thin: ATS scraper repos are 0 to 4 stars, and USAJOBS and H-1B tooling is similarly small. No curated, maintained dataset of ATS company slugs turned up. The tool would need user-supplied company lists plus URL-pattern detection. Because ATS market-share estimates are vendor-sourced and conflict (Workday 22.9% to 75.4%, Greenhouse 16.7% to 49.8%, depending on sample), supporting several vendors is safer than betting on one ([Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/99-percent-fortune-500-ats/), [ResumeAI](https://withresumeai.com/reports/state-of-ats-2026), [ResumeGeni](https://resumegeni.com/research/ats-market-share-2026)).

The US-specific candidate logic is where open source is nearly empty. Pay transparency is state law, not federal: sources count 13 to 18 states plus DC, including California, Colorado, New York, Washington and Illinois, with Virginia (1 July 2026) and Maine (29 July 2026) added recently ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-07-06-show-me-the-money-or-the-wage-range-new-state-pay-transparency-laws)). A parser should store currency and pay period and treat absent ranges as unknown. A sponsorship filter can be built from the DOL's free quarterly LCA disclosure files, but they exclude FEIN, so employer-name matching must be fuzzy, and an LCA is not an approved petition ([DOL OFLC](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/foreign-labor/performance), [Davidson Morris](https://www.davidsonmorris.com/h1b-database/)). Self-identification questions such as the disability form CC-305 are voluntary and confidential ([CC-305 copy](https://www.checkpoint.com/privacy/voluntary-self-identification-of-disability-form-cc-305/)). My design suggestion is to default to "decline to self-identify" and never fill a non-decline answer without explicit user input, and to require user confirmation for work-authorization and sponsorship answers. For resumes, blog consensus holds that US resumes carry no photo, use a single column, avoid tables and keep contact details in the body ([Careerminds](https://careerminds.com/blog/ats-compliant-resume)). I found no credible source on the one-versus-two-page rule. Existing open-source resume tools already cover this format, so ai-job-search's two-page CV rule needs checking against the user's seniority and not adopting blindly.

US legal risk points at contract and accounts, not criminal law. After Van Buren, the Ninth Circuit held in hiQ that "without authorization" does not apply to public websites, so scraping public pages is not a CFAA violation there ([Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/taking-cue-from-the-supreme-courts-van-buren-decision-ninth-circuit-releases-new-opinion-holding-scraping-of-publicly-available-website-data-falls-outside-of-cfaa)). But the 2022 hiQ outcome enforced LinkedIn's user agreement in contract and ended in a $500,000 consent judgment with data destruction ([Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case)). LinkedIn's later suit against Proxycurl ended in a permanent injunction and the company's shutdown ([LinkedIn News](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/LinkedInWinsLegalBattleToProtectMemberData)). In Meta v. Bright Data, the court read the terms as covering only logged-in use, and Meta dropped the rest of the case ([Eric Goldman](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2024/02/facebook-drops-anti-scraping-lawsuit-against-bright-data-guest-blog-post.htm)). The inference is that logged-out reads of public ATS endpoints and government APIs are the safe tier. Automating a logged-in LinkedIn session violates section 8.2 on its face, and the practical risk is account restriction. The AI-hiring rules mostly regulate employers and vendors: NYC Local Law 144 (a state audit called enforcement "ineffective" in Dec 2025, with penalties up to $1,500 per violation per day), California's FEHA automated-decision regulations (effective 1 Oct 2025), Illinois HB 3773 (1 Jan 2026) and a replaced, narrower Colorado law effective 1 Jan 2027 ([NY State Comptroller](https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2025/12/02/enforcement-local-law-144-automated-employment-decision-tools), [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/employee-rights-labour-relations/1673530/california-adopts-new-employment-ai-regulations-effective-october-1-2025), [Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/pub/what-colorado-ai-laws-major-rewrite-means-for-employers)). Mobley v. Workday shows liability attaching to employer-side vendors ([Duane Morris](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/2026/06/25/the-class-action-weekly-wire-episode-153-california-federal-court-grants-in-part-and-denies-in-part-motion-to-dismiss-in-algorithmic-bias-suit/)). I found no law or case restricting candidate-side auto-apply or resume tools, and that absence reflects my search, not a legal guarantee.

## Conclusion

The field has converged on a pattern that a French and US project can adopt directly: a prompt-and-file core, thin portal connectors with a stable `search`/`detail` contract, local storage, deterministic checks around LLM steps (posting integrity, PDF text-layer, fact verification), and a human-confirmed submit gate enforced in code. The 23,000 forks show how cheap localization is, yet the two markets this project targets are the ones where nobody has done the hard part. France's key data source closed to new users in October 2026 specifically because of AI reuse, so a French connector strategy must be built on La Bonne Alternance, CSP, ATS boards and user-driven page reads, with France Travail as an optional bring-your-own-key tier. The US has open, structured ATS endpoints but no maintained connector library.

The more durable differentiator is dual-market conventions encoded as data and checks, not prose. That means a French CV profile (photo optional, no état civil), vous-moi-nous letters, alternance and stage handling, brut annuel and Syntec parsing, versus US pay-range parsing, H-1B sponsor lookup and truthful, user-confirmed work-authorization and self-ID answers. Open questions to resolve before building: whether and how France Travail lifts its suspension, the actual French publication of employers on public ATS boards, primary-source texts for the Syntec, période d'essai and stage rules, and counsel's view on whether a candidate-side scorer touches the EU AI Act's employment category.
